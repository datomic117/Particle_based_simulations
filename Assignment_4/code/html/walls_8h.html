Model for a static vertical cylinder of radius R_cyl, centred on the axis of the box, that forms the silo wall above the bottom plate (Task C1).

Todo:
Task C1: the cylindrical silo wall. Its axis is the vertical line through (0.5*L.x, 0.5*L.y). With rc the distance of the particle centre to the axis, the particle overlaps the wall when rc > R_cyl - radius. The closest wall point is then at distance R_cyl from the axis, in the same direction as the particle, so riw is the horizontal vector (rc - R_cyl) * (dx, dy, 0) / rc, with (dx, dy) the horizontal offset of the centre from the axis, and riw.sq its square length. The wall is static: vw = 0. The cylinder only exists above the plate: return false for a particle with r->z < z_silo, so the discharged material can spread over the floor below. bottom_wall, at the top of walls.c, is an example of the contract.

Model for the silo bottom: a static horizontal plate at height z_silo with a circular orifice of radius R_orifice, centred on the axis, that is closed while the silo is filled and settles and opened at t_open (Task C2).

Todo:
Task C2: the edge of the hole. Here the orifice is open and the particle centre is over the hole (rc < R_orifice), within one radius of the plane of the plate. Such a particle can still touch the plate, at the edge of the hole. Work out which point of the plate is then closest to the centre, and return true when the particle overlaps it, with riw the vector from that point to the centre (riw.sq its square length) and vw = 0, as for every wall. Mind the particle that sits exactly on the axis. Until this is done, a particle over the hole never touches the plate: the silo discharges, but through a hole whose edge the particles do not feel.
